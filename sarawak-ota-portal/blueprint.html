One portal, many listings: putting Sarawak’s tourism operators into global distribution

The Cakrasoft ad shows the end state of a certified connectivity business. This is the route that gets there — starting with revenue in month two rather than certification in month eighteen.

The ask

What was requested

“One web portal that can become manager to the OTA — one web portal with the robot that can register a lot of listings, helping many businesses in Sarawak, then start doing the listings for them.”

That product is buildable, and it is the right ambition. But the path to it is not the path it looks like from the ad. Three constraints shape every decision below.

Constraints

Read this before scoping anything

Constraint 01 — Hard legal limit

The robot cannot register OTA accounts. Booking.com, Agoda, Expedia, Airbnb and Traveloka all prohibit automated account creation. Onboarding requires a verified legal entity, bank and payout details, a tax identity, and a contract signed by the owner.

A headless browser driving an extranet form is a terms-of-service breach that ends in banned accounts — and the account it burns belongs to the client, not to KOBIS. The robot never creates accounts. It prepares, validates and syncs content into accounts the operator legally owns.

Constraint 02 — Timeline

Direct connectivity is a 6–18 month certification, not an integration. The legitimate route into Booking.com is its Connectivity Partner Programme: apply, build against the Content, Rates & Availability and Reservations APIs, pass certification, commit to a minimum connected-property count, carry a support SLA. Expedia, Agoda YCS and Traveloka each run their own equivalent; Airbnb’s API is effectively closed to new partners for this use case.

So don’t build connectivity. Rent it.

Constraint 03 — Market

Sarawak’s underserved supply is not hotels. Kuching’s hotels are already on Booking.com and Agoda — they have revenue managers and channel managers. What is missing from global distribution is the experience layer: Bako and Semenggoh day trips, Batang Ai longhouse stays, Mulu and Niah caving, Miri diving, Rajang and Baram river trips, craft and food experiences, licensed guiding and 4WD charter.

Those sell on Klook, GetYourGuide, Viator, Airbnb Experiences and Traveloka Xperience — a different distribution stack from hotel inventory, with far lower barriers.

Recommendation

The shape of the answer

Build this Build a managed distribution portal for Sarawak experience operators. Rent channel connectivity from an aggregator. Point the AI at listing production and compliance — the work that actually blocks these operators — not at form-filling.

Connectivity

The defensible asset is not the API plumbing. It is the operator relationships, the listing quality, and the Sarawak inventory itself. This single decision converts an eighteen-month certification project into a six-week integration.

The robot

What the AI actually does

Not a form-filler — a listing production and compliance agent. This is where AI earns its place, because it removes the real blocker: Sarawak micro-operators have superb products and no way to produce distribution-grade content in five languages.

Intake

Photos and voice notes over WhatsApp, or uploaded through the portal, in Bahasa Malaysia, English, Iban or Chinese. No forms to get started.

Content generation

Claude produces — against a per-channel JSON schema, so field limits are never exceeded — title, short and long description, highlights, inclusions and exclusions, itinerary with durations, geocoded meeting point, what to bring, accessibility notes, age and fitness requirements, cancellation policy.

Translation

English, Bahasa Malaysia, 简体中文, 日本語 and 한국어 — the languages that actually drive Sarawak inbound.

Photo quality control

Resolution and aspect-ratio checks, per-channel hero-image rules, auto-crop to each channel’s spec, EXIF strip, generated alt text. Rejected photos come back with a plain-language reason.

Pricing intelligence

The operator states their net take-home; the robot grosses up per channel so a 25% Klook commission and an 18% Booking commission both land on the same net. Rate-parity conflicts are flagged before they breach a contract.

Compliance vault

MOTAC tourism operator licence, tourist guide licences, insurance certificates, SST registration, vessel permits — collected once, reused across every channel, with expiry reminders. For most operators this alone justifies the subscription.

Readiness scorecard

One percentage per channel with the exact blocking items listed. Nothing is pushed until it scores green.

Post-launch monitoring

Content drift, availability gaps, calendar sync, review-reply drafting, and booking alerts pushed to the operator’s WhatsApp.

A human approves every push. The robot drafts; KOBIS ops ships.

Architecture

How it is built

Next.js 15 with TypeScript and Tailwind on Netlify, matching the stack already used across this hub. Supabase for Postgres, Auth, Storage and Row Level Security — the isolation boundary between operators. Inngest or Trigger.dev for sync jobs, retries with backoff and per-channel rate limiting. Claude API with structured outputs bound to per-channel schemas. WhatsApp Business Cloud API for notifications, because it is the only channel these operators reliably read.

Security posture

Never store OTA passwords — API keys and OAuth tokens only, encrypted at rest. Listings are always created under the operator’s legal entity, with KOBIS added as an authorised user. Never under a KOBIS account: that inverts the liability and leaves operators unable to leave, which they will eventually resent.

Sequence

Four phases, each gated on the last

PHASE 0Weeks 1–4

Prove it by hand

Ten operators recruited through SEA. No portal at all. Intake in a shared sheet; listings created manually by KOBIS staff as authorised agents on the operators’ own Klook, GetYourGuide and Viator accounts.

Exit: real bookings from at least three operators. If this fails, no amount of software fixes it.

PHASE 1Months 2–4

Portal and robot

Next.js and Supabase. Onboarding wizard, AI content generation, translation, media pipeline, document vault, readiness scorecard, ops approval console. A single integration — Bókun — fanning out to Viator, GetYourGuide, Expedia and Airbnb Experiences.

Exit: 40 operators live, and a listing produced in under 30 minutes of human time.

PHASE 2Months 5–9

Direct channels and accommodation

Klook Merchant API direct. Channex.io for homestays and boutique lodging — the ad’s model, now that the roster and the ops discipline exist. Booking engine, WhatsApp notifications, payments, commission reconciliation.

Exit: 120 operators, and positive unit economics per operator.

PHASE 3Month 10+

The Sarawak marketplace

A consumer-facing storefront with unified availability and a package builder — Bako, then Semenggoh, then a longhouse night, sold as one basket. This is the position no OTA can replicate, because no OTA holds the whole Sarawak inventory in one place. Only here does building your own connectivity certifications start to make sense.

Exit: this is the destination, not a gate.

Economics

How it makes money

Most Sarawak micro-operators will not pay RM300 a month for software. Price for that reality rather than against it.

The override is what makes this work. It aligns KOBIS with operator success, survives operators who cannot afford a subscription, and scales with inventory rather than headcount. Phase 0 and Phase 1 cohorts are strong grant candidates — digitalising Sarawak tourism SMEs sits squarely inside the state’s digital economy agenda, and SEA is the recruitment channel.
