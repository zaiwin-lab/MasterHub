## 0. The one-paragraph thesis

Every SME owner in Malaysia has now heard that AI matters. Almost none of them know what
it means *for their business, this month, in ringgit*. That gap is the entire opportunity.
We do not sell AI — the market is already crowded with people selling AI and none of them
can price it, because nobody can value a thing that has no measured problem attached to it.
We sell **a measured problem and a fast fix**: we find the money already leaking out of a
business, put a number on it, and ship the smallest thing that stops the leak. AI is how we
do it in 30 days instead of 9 months. The MVP³ Potential Scan is the instrument that turns
a stranger into a prospect with a quantified problem — which is the only kind of prospect
that buys quickly and without haggling.

## 1. Category and positioning

### 1.1 The category decision

**Do not enter as an "AI consultancy."** That category is a red ocean with no price
integrity: the buyer cannot tell you apart from a freelancer with a ChatGPT subscription,
so the conversation collapses to day rates, and you compete on cheapness against people
with nothing to lose.

Enter instead as:

> **Business Potential Engineering** — we find the untapped MVP inside an existing business
> and take it to market before the competition wakes up.

This category has three commercial advantages:

| Advantage | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **ROI-anchored pricing** | The fee is justified against recovered leakage, not against hours. RM 4,800 against RM 22,000/month of leak is not a cost, it is arbitrage. |
| **No direct competitor** | Web agencies sell websites. AI shops sell chatbots. Management consultants sell decks. Nobody in Sarawak sells *"we find the hidden product and ship it."* |
| **Naturally recurring** | Every business has more than one MVP. The first engagement opens the second. |

### 1.2 Positioning statement

> For **Malaysian owner-led businesses with real revenue but stalled growth**,
> who **know AI matters but cannot see where it applies to them**,
> **SAIDS** is a **potential-engineering practice**
> that **finds the untapped product hiding in your operation and ships it in 30 days**.
> Unlike **AI consultants who sell you a strategy deck and disappear**,
> we **start with a free diagnostic that puts a ringgit figure on your leakage, and we build
> the fix ourselves.**

### 1.3 The named enemy

Every strong GTM needs an enemy that is *not a competitor*. Ours is:

> **The Comfortable Middle** — the business that is profitable enough not to panic and busy
> enough never to change. It does not fail. It gets quietly overtaken, and by the time the
> owner notices, the cost of catching up is 10× the cost of moving early.

Naming the enemy lets us apply pressure without attacking anyone. The prospect is not
being told they are bad at business — they are being told they are *standing still*, which
is a far easier thing for a proud owner-founder to admit.

### 1.4 Message hierarchy (use in this order, always)

1. **Every business has an untapped MVP.** (curiosity — the existing brand line, keep it)
2. **Yours is leaking money while you look for it.** (tension)
3. **8 minutes tells you where.** (low-friction resolution)
4. **We can stop the biggest leak in 30 days.** (capability)
5. **KAPT / KOBIS — business-first, human-directed, AI-accelerated.** (credibility)

**named person doing a repetitive thing more than 10 hours a week.** That is a leak with a
face on it, and the owner already feels it. The Scan is designed to surface exactly this.

## 3. Offer ladder

The mistake most consultancies make is one big offer with a cliff in front of it. We use a
five-rung ladder where each rung is an easy "yes" that makes the next rung obvious.

| Rung | Offer | Price | Duration | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | **MVP³ Potential Scan** | Free | 8 min | Capture + qualify + create the quantified problem |
| **1** | **Potential Readout** | RM 1,500 | 90 min live + 1-page written plan | Impulse tier. Closes *on the snapshot page* while the number is still hot. |
| **2** | **MVP³ Clarity Sprint** | RM 4,800 – 7,800 | 5 working days | The cash engine. Full diagnostic, leak-fix plan, clickable prototype of the MVP³ candidate. **100% credited into Rung 3.** |
| **3** | **SAIDS Launch Sprint** | RM 18,000 – 48,000 | 21–30 days | Working solution, live, in their hands. |
| **4** | **SAIDS Momentum** | RM 3,500 – 12,000/mo | Rolling | Operate, iterate, extend. The compounding revenue. |

### 3.1 Design notes that matter

- **Rung 1 exists to catch the impulse.** A snapshot that ends only in "book a call" loses
  the 15% who are ready to pay *right now*. RM 1,500 is under most owners' personal
  signing threshold — no approval, no procurement, no delay.
- **Rung 2 is the product to push.** Five days, high margin, AI-accelerated delivery,
  repeatable, and it pre-sells Rung 3 by construction. This is the "fast money" lane.
- **Crediting Rung 2 into Rung 3 removes the last objection** ("what if I pay for the
  diagnosis and then don't like the treatment"). It costs nothing — you were going to do
  the discovery work anyway.
- **Rung 4 is where the business actually becomes valuable.** Track it as the north star
  after month 6.

### 3.2 Risk reversal — the Leak Guarantee

> If the Clarity Sprint does not identify recoverable value worth at least **3× the fee**
> within 12 months, the fee is refunded in full.

This is safe to offer because the leaks are nearly always there — the Sprint's job is to
find and quantify them, and a business with none is a business we should not have sold to.
Print the guarantee on the pricing section, the proposal, and the invoice. It converts.

## 4. The engine: why the Scan is the whole GTM

Most consultancies market with content and hope. We market with an **instrument**. The Scan
does five jobs at once that would otherwise need five separate marketing systems:

| Job | How the Scan does it |
|---|---|
| **Lead capture** | Snapshot is delivered on-screen instantly; the email is asked for to *save and send* it, after the value has landed. |
| **Qualification** | Revenue band, headcount, decision authority and leak size are all answered inside the flow. Sales gets a scored, ranked queue. |
| **Urgency creation** | The Cost of Delay Clock converts their own answers into RM/month. |
| **Solution framing** | The Snapshot's "3 Moves" are written so that Moves 2 and 3 are precisely what a Clarity Sprint delivers. |
| **Proprietary data** | Every completed scan builds a benchmark dataset nobody else in Sarawak has. |

### 4.1 The compounding asset most people miss

At **N ≥ 50 completed scans** the aggregate becomes a publishable research asset:

> **The Sarawak Business Potential Index** — an annual report on AI readiness and
> operational leakage among Sarawak SMEs.

That report does four things at once: it earns press and state-agency attention (S2 door
opener), it makes every peer-comparison claim on the website *true and citable*, it makes
the Scan itself more valuable ("see how you compare to 400 Sarawak businesses"), and it
positions Zaiwin Kassim as the person who owns the data on this question in the state.

**This is the flywheel.** Scan → data → report → authority → more scans. Build for it from
day one: version the instrument, timestamp every response, store answers as stable keys
(not display strings) so the cohort stays comparable across copy edits and languages.

## 5. Urgency architecture — the fear that survives scrutiny

The brief asks for fear. Here is the strategic refinement that makes it work commercially:
**borrowed fear moves nobody; arithmetic fear closes.** A Malaysian SME owner has been
shown "AI is coming" statistics a hundred times and is now immune. What they have never
been shown is *their own number*. So we use macro statistics only to open the frame, then
pivot hard to their own arithmetic.

### 5.1 The Three-Beat Sequence

| Beat | Purpose | Where it lives | Emotional job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The wave is real** | Establish that this is not hype | Hero + one stats band, **3 numbers maximum** | "This is happening." |
| **2. The gap is widening** | Establish that peers are moving | Mid-page, benchmark strip | "Others are moving. I am not." |
| **3. The bill is yours** | Make it personal and unavoidable | **Inside the Snapshot only** | "This is costing me *now*." |

Beat 3 is where the sale is made, and it is the one thing competitors cannot copy without
building the same instrument.

### 5.2 The stats band — rules of engagement

Use **three** numbers on the site. More than three and each one gets weaker. Every number
must carry a visible source and year, and must link out. Unsourced statistics on a page
selling rigour will destroy the very credibility the page is trying to build — and a single
prospect who checks and finds it hollow will say so publicly.

**Candidate numbers, with confidence ratings. Verify each against the primary source and
capture the exact figure and URL before publishing:**

| Claim | Source to verify | Confidence | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~78% of organisations now use AI in at least one business function (up from ~55% a year earlier) | McKinsey, *The State of AI*, Mar 2025 | High — figure widely reported, confirm exact % and survey year | Beat 1: the wave is real |
| ~95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots deliver no measurable P&L impact | MIT NANDA, *The State of AI in Business 2025* | High on existence, **verify exact framing** — it is often misquoted | Beat 1 + our differentiator: *pilots fail, shipped products don't* |
| Only ~1 in 4 companies get past proof-of-concept to real value | BCG, *Where's the Value in AI?* (2024) | Medium — confirm the exact ratio | Beat 2: the gap is widening |
| SMEs are ~97% of Malaysian business establishments and ~39% of GDP | DOSM / SME Corp Malaysia | High on magnitude, verify current year | Relevance to the reader |
| Malaysia's National AI Office (NAIO) and national AI action plan | MOSTI / NAIO official releases | Medium — confirm dates and current plan period | "Policy is moving, you should be too" |
| Sarawak Digital Economy Strategy 2030 / PCDS 2030 targets | SDEC / Sarawak Premier's Dept. | Medium — confirm current targets | Local proof for S2 |

> **Hard rule for the build:** no statistic ships without a source line and a working link.
> If a number cannot be verified in 10 minutes, cut it. Two verified numbers beat five
> impressive ones.

### 5.3 The lines that carry the fear

Written to press without insulting a proud owner-founder. The pressure is on *time*, never
on their competence.

- **"The wave doesn't wait for the undecided."**
- **"You are not behind. You are still, and still is the new behind."**
- **"Late is expensive. Still is worse — late can be bought back, still compounds."**
- **"Your competitor doesn't need to be better than you. They only need to be earlier."**
- **"In 8 minutes you'll know what standing still costs you per month. Then it's your call."**
- **"Every month you wait, the same leak bills you again."**

### 5.4 Guardrail — do not fake the peer comparison

Until N ≥ 50 real responses, Beat 2 must be phrased from **public sources only**
("Malaysian SMEs reporting AI use rose from X to Y — MDEC 2025"). It must **never** say
"businesses like yours scored 61" while that number is invented. The FAME build already
enforces this discipline; carry it over. When the real cohort exists, Beat 2 becomes the
strongest section on the site — and it will be *true*.

## 6. The Snapshot — what the respondent receives

The Snapshot is the product. It must be good enough that people forward it to their
business partner. That forward is the cheapest lead source we will ever have.

| Block | Content | Sales job |
|---|---|---|
| **MVP³ Index** | 0–100 + band (Dormant / Stirring / Building / Compounding / Market-Ready) | Ego hook — a score demands comparison |
| **Three lenses** | Sub-scores: **Build Readiness** · **Hidden Potential** · **Market Pull** | Makes MVP³ a diagnostic tool, not a slogan |
| **Leakage Ledger** | Top 3 leaks, each with an estimated RM or hours/month, **with the arithmetic shown** | The number they cannot unsee |
| **Cost of Delay Clock** | Estimated RM at risk over the next 90 days if nothing changes | Beat 3 — converts to urgency |
| **The First 7** | Three fixes they can do themselves this week, free | Reciprocity. Proves we are not gatekeeping. |
| **The Next 30 / Next 90** | Two moves that require a partner | This *is* the Clarity Sprint scope |
| **Your MVP³ candidate** | One named product/service opportunity hiding in their operation | The "I never thought of that" moment — highest-converting single element |
| **SAIDS fit** | Which SAIDS module applies, with the specific KAPT capability named | Makes the offer concrete |
| **Honesty panel** | Declared vs. verified, how estimates were calculated, what was not measured | The trust differentiator (see §7) |

### 6.1 The Leakage Ledger — six named leaks

This taxonomy is the intellectual property. Name them, trademark the language, use the
names consistently across the site, the Snapshot, the proposals and the sales calls.

| Code | Leak | Estimated from | Typical SAIDS fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** | **Response Lag** — enquiries not answered fast enough | leads/month × est. miss rate × avg deal value × close rate | AI first-responder on WhatsApp |
| **L2** | **Manual Repetition** — hours on work a machine should do | hours/week × 4.33 × loaded hourly cost | Workflow automation |
| **L3** | **Invisible Offer** — real capability nobody can find | declared assets vs. discoverability answers | Discoverable digital presence |
| **L4** | **Quote Drag** — days between enquiry and proposal | avg days × pipeline value × decay rate | Instant quote/proposal engine |
| **L5** | **One-Head Dependency** — knowledge locked in the founder | founder-dependency answers | Knowledge base + assisted SOPs |
| **L6** | **Untapped Asset** — data, audience or IP not monetised | asset inventory vs. revenue lines | The MVP³ candidate itself |

**Estimation discipline:** every figure is an estimate built from *their declared inputs*,
labelled as an estimate, with the formula visible on demand. Never present an estimate as a
measurement. Paradoxically, showing the arithmetic makes the number *more* persuasive — the
owner mentally checks it against what they know, agrees, and now owns the conclusion.

## 7. Proof and trust architecture

The hardest GTM problem for KAPT is that a small, new team is claiming enterprise-grade
capability. Standard fixes (logos, testimonials, headcount) are not available yet. Use
these four instead — all available immediately, all more convincing than a logo wall.

1. **Radical transparency as a weapon.** The FAME build already ships *"Show the structured
   output behind this report"*, classifies every finding as fact / inference /
   recommendation / limitation, and refuses to fabricate a single number. Almost nobody in
   this market does that. Make it a headline claim, not a footnote: **"We show our working.
   Ask any other AI vendor to show theirs."**
2. **Publish the instrument.** Put the scoring weights, the leak formulas and the version
   history on a public `/method` page. Vendors who are bluffing cannot copy this, because
   they have nothing behind the curtain.
3. **The portfolio is the proof.** github.com/zaiwin-lab already carries real, inspectable
   builds. For technical and institutional buyers (S2), a working repository outranks any
   brochure. Link it prominently from `/method` and `/about` — and note that S1 buyers will
   never click it, so it must never occupy prime homepage real estate.
4. **Named human accountability.** "Business-first. Human-directed. AI-accelerated." with a
   real face, real name, MBA, real company (KOBIS Berhad, www.kobisberhad.com). In a market
   nervous about faceless AI vendors, a named, credentialed, locally-present principal is a
   moat.

## 8. Channels and funnel

### 8.1 Two front doors — do not merge them

| Door | Audience | Asset | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **saids.kobisberhad.com** (or `mvp3.my`) | S1 + S3 buyers | The portal + Scan | Convert |
| **github.com/zaiwin-lab** | S2 evaluators, partners, talent | Portfolio, method, instrument | Verify |

The buyer's journey runs **Door 1 → decide**. The evaluator's runs **Door 1 → Door 2 →
Door 1**. Never send an SME owner to GitHub; never send a procurement evaluator away
without a link to it.

### 8.2 Channel priority for the first 90 days

| Rank | Channel | Why now | First action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Direct WhatsApp to warm network** | Zero cost, highest trust, fastest signal. 40 real conversations tells you more than any ad budget. | Personal message + Scan link to 40 known owners |
| 2 | **LinkedIn (Zaiwin's own voice)** | S2 and S3 live here. Founder-led content outperforms brand pages by an order of magnitude. | 3 posts/week: one leak teardown, one stat with commentary, one Scan finding (anonymised) |
| 3 | **Chambers, associations, trade bodies** | One talk = 60 qualified S1 leads in a room, with borrowed authority | Offer a free 45-min talk: *"The 6 leaks in every Sarawak SME"* — ends with the QR to the Scan |
| 4 | **Referral from Rung 2/3 clients** | Highest-converting channel once it exists | Ask at delivery, every time, with a named incentive |
| 5 | **Paid (Meta/Google)** | Only *after* the funnel converts organically | Do not spend until Scan→call ≥ 15% |

> **Do not start with paid ads.** Paid traffic into an unvalidated funnel converts a
> marketing budget into a learning expense. Get the first 100 organic scans, fix the drop-off
> points, *then* buy traffic — the same budget will return 3–5× more.

### 8.3 Funnel model — three honest scenarios

Model assumptions to instrument and then correct with real data. Do not treat as forecasts.

| Stage | Rate | Seed (mo 1–3) | Traction (mo 4–8) | Scale (mo 9+) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scan starts / month | — | 120 | 350 | 1,000 |
| Completion | 60% | 72 | 210 | 600 |
| Email captured | 75% of completes | 54 | 158 | 450 |
| Call booked | 20% of completes | 14 | 42 | 120 |
| Rung 1 or 2 closed | 30% of calls | 4 | 13 | 36 |
| Rung 2 revenue @ RM 4,800 | — | **RM 19,200** | **RM 62,400** | **RM 172,800** |
| Escalation to Rung 3 | 30% | 1.2 | 4 | 11 |
| Rung 3 revenue @ RM 28,000 avg | — | **RM 33,600** | **RM 112,000** | **RM 308,000** |
| **Monthly total (ex-retainer)** | | **~RM 53,000** | **~RM 174,000** | **~RM 481,000** |

**The two numbers that decide everything:** *completion rate* and *completes → call*. If
completion is under 50%, the survey is too long or too heavy — cut questions, do not add
persuasion. If completes → call is under 12%, the Snapshot is not landing the pain — the
Leakage Ledger and Cost of Delay Clock need to hit harder, not the CTA.

## 10. Metrics

Track weekly. Five numbers, nothing more — a dashboard nobody reads is worse than no dashboard.

| Metric | Definition | Target by day 90 | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scan completion** | completes ÷ starts | ≥ 60% | Is the instrument the right weight |
| **Snapshot → call** | calls booked ÷ completes | ≥ 20% | Is the pain landing |
| **Call → paid** | any rung closed ÷ calls | ≥ 30% | Is the offer priced and framed right |
| **Median leak found** | median RM/month across all snapshots | ≥ RM 8,000 | Is the ledger credible and material |
| **Sprint delivery hours** | hours per Clarity Sprint | ↓ 40% by Sprint #4 | Is the margin compounding |

Instrument these from day one — anonymous scan ID, funnel step events, drop-off question ID.
Without drop-off-by-question you cannot fix completion, and completion is the top of everything.

## 11. Risks and guardrails

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Unverified statistics get challenged publicly** | High | §5.2 hard rule: source line + working link, or cut |
| **Leak estimates read as fabricated** | High | Show the arithmetic; label estimates as estimates; never state a measurement we did not take |
| **Survey too long → completion collapses** | High | Cap at 22 questions / 8 minutes. Autosave + resume. Instrument drop-off by question ID and cut ruthlessly. |
| **Delivery cannot keep pace with sales** | Medium | Cap concurrent Rung 3 builds at 2 until the Sprint is productised. A missed deadline in a small market costs more than the revenue was worth. |
| **Fear-first tone alienates proud owner-founders** | Medium | Pressure applies to *time and market*, never to their competence. Every fear beat is followed within one screen by an achievable move. |
| **Over-building the portal → paralysis** | Medium | Scope frozen in the build prompt. Ship v1 in 14 days. No auth, no dashboard, no CMS, no live LLM in v1. |
| **PDPA / data handling on collected business data** | Medium | Explicit consent screen (reuse FAME's pattern), stated retention period, no sensitive personal data, named data controller |
| **Claiming partnerships not yet approved** | High | Reuse FAME's `branding.ts` flag pattern — no partner name, logo or endorsement renders until written approval is held |

## 12. What we are deliberately not doing in v1

Stated explicitly, because scope discipline is what gets this live in 14 days instead of 14 weeks.

Client login and dashboards · a live LLM behind the assistant · automated PDF generation on a
server · a blog or CMS · paid traffic · case-study library (there are none yet) ·
multi-currency · CRM integration beyond an email list and a WhatsApp handoff · verified
fetching of submitted links · an admin analytics UI (read the raw table).

Each of these is a Phase 2 decision to be made *after* the funnel has produced 100 real
scans and told us what is actually broken.
